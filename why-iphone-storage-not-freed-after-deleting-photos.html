Why iPhone Storage Isn't Freed After Deleting Photos

Updated October 2026 · By the Fotik developer

Short answer. When you delete a photo, iOS moves it to Recently Deleted and keeps it for 30 days, still using storage. To free the space, open Photos > Albums > Recently Deleted and delete the items there. Fotik shows what is waiting and what is freed as two separate numbers.

Step by step

  1. Delete as usualDelete photos in Fotik or in Photos. iOS moves them to Recently Deleted.
  2. Open Recently DeletedIn Photos open Albums, scroll to Utilities and tap Recently Deleted (Fotik has an Open Recently Deleted button).
  3. Delete them permanentlyTap Select, then Delete All and confirm. The files are removed for good.
  4. Tell FotikFotik cannot see inside that album, so tap I Emptied the Trash to reset its count.

Frequently asked questions

Why does iOS keep deleted photos?

As a safety net: anything you delete stays recoverable for 30 days.

Can an app empty Recently Deleted for me?

No. Apps cannot clear it programmatically, which is why Fotik shows the wait instead of claiming the space is freed.

How long until space comes back automatically?

After the 30 days, iOS removes the items itself.

Why is my storage still full after emptying it?

Check Settings > General > iPhone Storage; the number can take a moment to update and other data such as apps and system files also counts.

Does Fotik verify I emptied the album?

No. It cannot see the album, so it takes your word when you tap I Emptied the Trash.

Do other cleaner apps have the same limit?

Yes, every app on iOS is bound by the same Recently Deleted rule.
